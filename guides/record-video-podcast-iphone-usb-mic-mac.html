How to record a video podcast with an iPhone camera and a USB mic on your Mac

By Mohsin Sapra · Published October 8, 2026 · About 5 min read

A video podcast needs two things that live in different places. The best camera you own is on your iPhone. The best microphone you own is probably a USB or XLR podcast mic plugged into your Mac.

Normally you would record both separately and sync them by hand. CamBridge 1.2.1 does the sync for you.

Quick answer

In Studio, open the MAC MIC section and pick your USB mic. Every iPhone recording now also records the mic on the Mac. When the clip arrives you get <clip> (Mac mic).mov: the untouched iPhone video with your Mac mic as the only audio. Keep the iPhone mic on, because it is the sync reference.

Why record the mic on the Mac

The iPhone's rear camera beats most webcams by a wide margin. A dedicated USB or podcast mic beats the phone's built-in mic by a similar margin. Using both gives you a video podcast that looks and sounds professional, without buying a camera.

The catch is that the two recordings start at slightly different moments and run on different clocks. Over a long episode they drift apart. CamBridge handles this by matching the Mac audio to the iPhone mic audio, then correcting for drift, so the finished clip stays in sync from start to end.

Step by step with CamBridge

You need CamBridge 1.2.1 or later on both devices, a USB or podcast microphone connected to the Mac, and an iPhone on a tripod.

  1. Plug the mic into the Mac

    Connect your USB or podcast microphone. Set it up as you normally do, with the gain at a safe level.

  2. Open the MAC MIC section in Studio

    Choose your mic from the list. A level meter shows the signal, so you can check that it moves when you speak.

  3. Keep the iPhone mic on

    The iPhone's own microphone records too. Do not turn it off. CamBridge uses it as a reference to line the Mac audio up with the video.

  4. Frame the shot

    Mount the iPhone, connect over Wi‑Fi or USB, and check the preview in Studio. Click to focus and set exposure. For a vertical clip, set portrait capture orientation.

  5. Record

    Press Space in Studio. The iPhone records 4K, and the Mac records your mic at the same time.

  6. Stop and wait for the transfer

    The video file copies to the Footage folder. Then CamBridge builds the synced clip.

  7. Use the synced file

    Open <clip> (Mac mic).mov. It holds the untouched iPhone video with your Mac mic as the only audio. Sync is done by audio, with drift correction. The raw WAV from the Mac is kept too, if you want to edit the sound separately.

Without CamBridge

  • Clap sync. Record the camera and mic separately and line them up with a clap or waveform in your editor. Free, and it works, but it is manual and drifts on long recordings.
  • Wire the mic to the iPhone. A USB or Lightning adapter lets the phone record the mic directly. You lose the Mac-side recording flow. See the DJI Mic 2 guide for wireless options.
  • Continuity Camera with a podcast app. The Mac app picks the camera and the mic. You record a webcam-quality stream rather than the iPhone's 4K file.
  • Dedicated recorders. Record audio on a separate device and sync in the edit. Common in studios.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • Do a 10 second test and play back the synced clip before a long session.
  • Leave the iPhone mic where it can hear you. A muted or covered mic makes it harder to sync.
  • Do not unplug the USB mic mid-recording.
  • For guests in the same room, record each person on their own phone. See multi-iPhone recording.

Limits to know about

The Mac mic feature is in 1.2.1 and is a beta feature. The synced clip appears after the transfer, not live. The iPhone mic must stay on as the sync reference. It records one Mac mic input at a time. It does not record a live mix of several mics.

FAQ

Which mics work?

USB and podcast microphones the Mac recognizes, listed in Studio's MAC MIC section.

Is my original iPhone video kept?

Yes. The file named (Mac mic) uses the untouched iPhone video. The original recording is also kept.

How is the audio synced?

Automatically, by matching the Mac audio to the iPhone mic audio, with drift correction.

Can I get the raw audio?

Yes. The raw WAV is kept.

Do I need the cable?

No. Wi‑Fi or USB both work for the live link.

Can I use this for a live stream?

No. CamBridge records files. It is not a virtual camera.
